# 0002 Je m'arrête pas — lyrics

Her side of the anniversary year (2025-10-04 to 2026-10-03), and the gift of the three. Sung by a female voice narrating her own year, not him addressing her: an accurate inventory of what she carried without being asked says more than any declaration, and it keeps clear of a love song. Fun, warm, wry. Not sad.

A, B and C were written first, and C was rendered in Suno. D was written after, for a faster sound she also likes, and D shipped.

## What every version shares

**March 8 is off-limits.** The euthanasia, the hours at the vet, the crying: none of it appears in any version. It's a sad scene, this is a fun song, and the point is not to make her cry. Nothing here refers to how he died.

**Daru lives in the red pouch instead.** His ashes came home from the vet in a plastic bag, and she moved them into a red shisa face pouch: plush, yellow mane, blue spiral eyes, two little fangs, a long shoulder strap. By day it sits in Daru's old dog house, otherwise empty. Every night she carries it upstairs and puts it next to her to sleep. That object and that ritual do the whole job; grief is there without a sad line.

**She's picked up a few shisa because they look like him.** A few, not a shelf. The French says `des shisa qui lui ressemblent` and that's enough. Never gloss it as a statue.

**Two lines carry the set:**

- « L'un dort sur le carrelage / l'autre dort à côté de moi »: one sleeps on the tiles, the other sleeps next to me. In A's chorus.
- « L'un me réveille, l'autre me manque »: one wakes me, the other I miss. The last line of the bridge in A, C and D.

## A — L'Ombre et l'Étoile

**The two dogs, named as she named them.** A waltz, warm, the most beautiful of the four. The title is from the registered names she chose in 2010: *D'Artagnan L'Ombre d'Argent* and *Aramis L'Étoile Filante*, the Shadow and the Star. One is a shadow now; the other is a shooting star at fifteen. Neither fact is ever stated.

An alternate title is « Le lion rouge », with the chorus's first line swapped to `Dans le lion rouge`: a song called *The Red Lion* that turns out to be about a plush bag with a dog inside it.

The ants went on to C's second chorus and D's pre-chorus, and the musketeer names are A's bridge.

## C — Debout dans la cuisine

**Her stolen minutes, and the neighbourhood on the dog walk.** House scenes alternate with a walk that comes back three times (verses 3, 5 and 7). The genre is French house nu-disco because she likes Daft Punk; Suno strips artist names, so the Style field names the ingredients. Vocoder on the backing vocals only.

This is the take that exists in Suno, made from exactly this prompt. D replaced it as the faster, more defiant song. Words like `chanson`, `ballad`, `minimal`, `sparse`, `late-night` and `intimate` pull this sound sad, so they stay out of it.

How C is built:

- **Seven verses, three choruses, a bridge.** Each walk opens `Je sors le chien`. Choruses 1 and 2 keep `Debout dans la cuisine / Deux minutes à moi` and change the last two lines. Chorus 3 keeps only the title line, then turns to October 4.
- **The three walks share one melody.** Verse 7's last line is short on purpose.
- **Chorus 2:** the boy is in a back carrier, too heavy, not in her arms. The ants never stop.
- **Verse 2:** the girl is bad at the Switch and still runs it. Mahjong is hers and gets interrupted.
- **Verse 4** is the laugh-out-loud comedy, at any hour, in bed too. Anime is at midnight.
- **The last walk ends `j'ai pas le temps pour Dieu`.** Spoken French throughout: `j'ai pas`, `ils restent jamais`.
- **The neighbours stay in silhouette,** no nationality, no faces. The fight is heard, not seen.
- **The bridge is the pouch ritual.** She doesn't wear it. `des shisa`, never statue, statuette or figurine. No halo on Daru.
- **The test for a French ear:** *est-ce que ça sonne comme une chanson d'amour ?* If yes, it failed. The final chorus most of all.
- Not in it: a weight line, March 8, « je t'aime », « mon amour », « pour toujours ». It isn't a translation of 0003 and shares no scene with it.

## D — Je m'arrête pas (shipped)

**Same year, opposite engine.** C is her noticing things; D is her not stopping. The whole song is one echo: the ants never stop, and neither does she. `Ça s'arrête jamais` is a complaint in the pre-chorus, and `je m'arrête pas` is a boast in the chorus: the same words turned around.

**This is as much hers as C is.** She likes Daft Punk, which is C's genre, and Japanese anime and its theme songs, which is D's. Neither is a concession to the video.

**The structure comes from the 怪物 reference, not just the tempo.** 170 BPM, flat held-back verses, a pre-chorus that builds, a belted chorus, a keys-led bridge, and a final chorus lifted a tone. Eight sung sections against C's eleven: at this speed a longer song is exhausting.

**Aggressive, not dark.** The lyric is combative and so is the vocal, but the reference's minor-key gloom stays out. Self-pity at 170 BPM is a dirge with a fast drummer. She's winning, not suffering.

Lyric and Suno prompt: [README.md](README.md#song). The stills follow D's scenes; C's gelato, Switch, mahjong and 3am milk stay out of them. The pictures cut through verse 2 and don't act out `je ralentis exprès`.

Notes on the wording:

- **The chorus makes a claim, not a complaint.** `Essaie de m'arrêter`, try and stop me, then `je suis pas fatiguée / je suis en train de gagner`: she isn't tired, she's winning. The first draft of D ran C's observations faster and kept C's `deux minutes`, and *I take my two minutes* is too small a claim to shout at 170 BPM. A line in D that sounds wry rather than defiant is C bleeding through.
- **No `ne` anywhere, title included.** `Je m'arrête pas` is five syllables against six and it's how she'd say it. Dropping the particle stops a French lyric sounding recited: `ça s'arrête pas`, `personne me demande`, `j'y touche pas`, `personne y reste`, `m'a jamais`, `j'en ai rien à foutre`.
- **The last chorus turns on `sauf ce soir`**, except tonight. A song that spends three minutes refusing to stop admits exactly one exception, and it's the anniversary.
- **`c'est gagné` answers `je suis en train de gagner`.** Winning becomes won, same verb. Not `j'ai gagné`: French gives age with *avoir*, so `vingt et un ans — j'ai gagné` can be heard as an age. `C'est gagné` has the same seven syllables and can only mean *we've pulled it off*.
- **`deux minutes` never appears.** It's C's title and thesis; negating it in D would answer a question D never asks.
- **Both pre-choruses have one shape, short / short / long / long** (5 / 6 / 11 / 9 and 5 / 7 / 9 / 9), so Suno writes one melody for the slot and the build reads as a build. The first one carries the thesis: `les fourmis reviennent par le même trou / et moi je remonte l'escalier`, the ants come back through the same hole and she goes back up the same stairs.
- **Only two clock times.** Verse 1 says `six heures`, verse 2 says `midi`. A third time in the God pre-chorus sent the song back to 7am, so it became `Encore eux. Sur mon perron.`: the evangelists are one more thing that keeps coming back, the same joke as the ants.
- **One `ils` per set of neighbours.** `Personne y reste` takes the pronoun off the corner house's tenants, so `ils s'engueulent` can only be the couple fighting.
- **Verse lines run 12–16 syllables, dense on purpose.** Uneven lines, not long ones, are what make Suno cram or drop words. Verse 2's last line is the longest at 16: the open window, the fight and `exprès` can't lose anything.
- **Hard stops are rhythm.** `Fenêtre ouverte. Ils s'engueulent. Je ralentis exprès.` is three punches, and it mirrors verse 1's opening. Style asks for clipped staccato verses to get them delivered that way.
- **The God lines are the most aggressive in the set, and she's why they're allowed.** `Ton Dieu m'a jamais gardé les gosses / j'en ai rien à foutre de ton Dieu`: your God never watched my kids, I don't give a fuck about your God. `Sur mon perron` matters: they came to her door, so the refusal is fair. Without the swear, the first line still lands on its own.
- **`je ralentis exprès`**, I slow down on purpose. Listening in on the fight is a decision, not something that happens to her; for the same reason it's `s'engueulent`, not `se disputent`, and `bagnole`, not `voiture`.
- **No species in the lyric.** In the pictures she's human and everyone else can be an animal. The pouch, `un petit lion rouge`, is the one animal object, with no teeth or prey language on top.
- **Verse 1 is the 6am machine,** four lines without a pause, ending on `personne me demande si j'ai dormi`. Aramis is too old to scratch the door, so he cries in the kitchen; she drinks tea, not coffee; the boy wants おんぶ (`mon dos`), not 抱っこ (`mes bras`). The pictures don't put him on her back: carrying him through the fights would glue a second body onto every action shot.
- **Verse 2 packs the four walk sightings into four lines**, where C spread them over three walks.
- **`ma fille veut tout décider`**, not `veut la manette`: the daughter throwing the controller is 0003's. Not `veut commander` either, which reads as ordering food.
- **`veut mon dos` reads right to a French ear and wrong to a translator.** French has no word for おんぶ, but `vouloir les bras` is how a small child asks to be picked up, so `veut mon dos` reads as *wants to be carried on my back*. A machine renders it as "wants my back".
- **The bridge is the only place it breathes**, a keys-led breakdown before the last chorus. `electronic keys`, not acoustic piano, which leaves piano to 0003.
- **`sa niche vide`, not `sa vieille maison`.** D's bridge introduces the pouch cold, and *his old house* could be a real house. `Niche` can only be a doghouse, and `vide` says it's empty otherwise.
- **The bridge opens on `il`**, and for one line it sounds like Aramis, the only dog named so far. `Un petit lion rouge` then reveals it isn't.
- **The final chorus runs 5 / 9 / 7 / 8** against 5 / 6 / 6 / 7: the lifted last pass expands, as the reference does.
- **`key change` isn't excluded**, because the reference lifts the last chorus a whole tone. `sentimental` and `orchestral strings` still block a power-ballad swell. `slap bass` is excluded: the reference's low end is a droning synth bass. `Japanese lyrics` is excluded too, because a J-rock tag pulls Japanese vowels onto a French vocal.
