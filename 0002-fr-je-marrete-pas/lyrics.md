Sung by a **female voice, narrating her year** — not him addressing her. An accurate inventory of what she carried for a year without being asked says more than any declaration, and it doesn't trip the love-song rule.

## Two rules for this song

**1. March 8 is off-limits.** The euthanasia, the hours at the vet, the crying — none of it appears in any version and none of it should. It's a sad scene, this is a fun song, and the point is not to make her cry. Nothing below refers to how he died.

**2. Daru lives in the red pouch instead.** His ashes came in a plastic bag from the vet and she moved them into a **red shisa face pouch** — plush, yellow mane, blue spiral eyes, two little fangs, long shoulder strap.

**And there's a daily ritual attached to it.** By day the pouch sits in **Daru's old dog house**, which is otherwise empty. Every night she carries it upstairs and puts it next to her to sleep. Down in the morning, up at night, 365 times this year.

That object and that ritual do the entire job. Grief is present without a sad line. C and D both end the bridge on `L'un me réveille, l'autre me manque`.

She's also picked up a few shisa because they look like him. A few — not a shelf, not a hoard. The French says `des shisa qui lui ressemblent` and that's enough; **don't gloss it as a statue.** They're small goods, and the word carries itself.

Two lines recur across all three versions. If you keep nothing else, keep these:

- **« L'un dort sur le carrelage / l'autre dort à côté de moi »** — one sleeps on the tiles, the other sleeps next to me. Takes a second to land, then doesn't leave.
- **« L'un me réveille, l'autre me manque »** — one wakes me, the other I miss.

## Version D — « Je m'arrête pas » ← **locked**

**Same year, opposite engine.** C is her noticing things; D is her not stopping. The thesis is one echo: the ants never stop, and neither does she — so `ça s'arrête jamais` is a complaint in the pre-chorus and `je m'arrête pas` is a boast in the chorus, the same words turned around.

**The first draft of D failed by inheriting C's register.** It ran C's observations faster, which isn't the same thing as a different song — `deux minutes dans la cuisine` is C's own title and thesis, and at 170 BPM with a belted chorus, *I take my two minutes* is too small a claim to shout. The chorus now makes a real one: `je suis pas fatiguée / je suis en train de gagner` — I'm not tired, I'm winning. **That's the aggressive reframe of the whole year**, and it's hers: she's not the patient one, she's the one who wins. If a line in D sounds wry rather than defiant, it's C bleeding through and it should be cut.

**This is as much hers as C is.** She likes Daft Punk, which is C's genre, and she likes Japanese anime and its theme songs, which is D's. Neither version is a concession to the video.

**Written for the 怪物 reference, and the structure is copied from it, not just the tempo.** 170 BPM, flat withheld verses, a `[Pre-Chorus]` that builds, a belted chorus, a keys-led bridge, then a final chorus lifted a tone. Eight sung sections against C's eleven — at this speed the song has to be shorter or it's exhausting.

**Aggressive, yes. Dark, no.** The lyric is now genuinely combative and the vocal should be, but the reference's minor-key gloom is still the wrong import — self-pity at 170 BPM is a dirge with a fast drummer. She's winning, not suffering. Keep the engine and the teeth, skip the gloom.

**`slap bass` is excluded, not requested.** The real low end on that record is a droning synth bass; slap is the word that dated the JP prompt.

Lyric and Suno prompt as shipped: [README.md](README.md#song).

### Notes — Version D

- **The chorus is a boast, not a complaint.** `Essaie de m'arrêter` — try and stop me. Then the reframe: she isn't exhausted, she's ahead. C spends eleven sections noticing how little she gets; D spends eight refusing the premise.
- **No `ne` anywhere, title included.** `Je m'arrête pas` is five syllables against six and it's how she'd say it; dropping the negative particle is the cheapest way to stop a French lyric sounding recited, and at this tempo the saved syllable is real. Same throughout: `ça s'arrête pas`, `personne me demande`, `j'y touche pas`, `personne y reste`, `m'a jamais`, `j'en ai rien à foutre`.
- **The last chorus turns on `sauf ce soir` — except tonight.** A song that spends three minutes refusing to stop admits exactly one exception, and it's the anniversary.
- **`deux minutes` must not appear in D at all.** The first rewrite left `pas deux minutes` in the final chorus, which was dead weight from the old chorus: the phrase is C's title and C's thesis, and D never says it, so negating it answers a question this song never asked. **A callback needs something to call back to.** The last chorus now flips D's own line instead — `je suis en train de gagner` in the choruses becomes `c'est gagné` at the end. Present to past, winning to won, same verb.
- **`c'est gagné`, never `j'ai gagné`, and the reason is the age idiom.** French states age with *avoir*: `j'ai vingt et un ans` = I'm twenty-one. Writing `vingt et un ans — j'ai gagné` puts `ans` and `j'ai` side by side, which is that idiom's exact shape, and at 170 BPM a listener can hear an age instead of an anniversary. Nobody in this family is 21, so there's no real person to confuse it with, but the line still muddles. `C'est gagné` is the same seven syllables, is idiomatic for *we've pulled it off*, and cannot be parsed as anyone's age.
- **Both pre-choruses share one shape — short / short / long / long.** PC1 is 5 / 6 / 11 / 9, PC2 is 5 / 7 / 9 / 9, close enough that Suno writes a single melody for the slot instead of two unrelated ones, which is what makes the build read as a build. **Keep them matched if you edit either.** PC1 earns its long lines: `les fourmis reviennent par le même trou / et moi je remonte l'escalier` — the ants come back through the same hole, she goes back up the same stairs. That's the thesis in two lines.
- **The clock had to go from the God pre-chorus.** Verse 1 stamps `six heures`, verse 2 stamps `midi`, and the draft's `sept heures` then sent the song backwards to 7am after it had already reached noon. Stamp three times in one lyric and listeners track them. `Encore eux. Sur mon perron.` fixes it and is the better line anyway: *them again* makes the evangelists one more thing that keeps coming back, which is the same joke as the ants.
- **`ils` meant two different sets of neighbours in consecutive lines.** The corner house's revolving tenants, then the couple fighting through the open window. `Personne y reste` takes the pronoun off the first one so `ils s'engueulent` can only be the second.
- **Verses run 12–16 syllables a line, dense on purpose.** At 170 BPM the reference's verses are fast and wordy, but *wildly uneven* lines are what makes Suno cram or silently drop words — the first draft's 14 / 10 / 13 / 12 was the problem, not the length. **Verse 2's last line is the longest in the song at 16** and stays that way: the open window, the fight and `exprès` are three things the line can't lose, and hard stops give a wordy line room. It's the one line to check for dropped words on take one. If you rewrite a verse line, count it.
- **Hard stops are doing rhythmic work, not punctuation work.** `Fenêtre ouverte. Ils s'engueulent. Je ralentis exprès.` is three punches where the draft had one comma-spliced sentence, and it mirrors verse 1's opening. Style asks for clipped staccato verses to get this delivered rather than smoothed.
- **The God line is the most aggressive thing in the set, and she's the reason it's allowed.** `Ton Dieu m'a jamais gardé les gosses / j'en ai rien à foutre de ton Dieu` — your God never watched my kids, I don't give a fuck about your God. The argument lands harder than the swear does, and keeping both is what makes it her rather than just profanity. `Sur mon perron` is load-bearing: they came to her door, so the refusal is fair rather than gratuitous. **If the swear has to go, cut the second line, not the first** — `ton Dieu m'a jamais gardé les gosses` still detonates into the chorus on its own.
- **`je ralentis exprès`** — I slow down on purpose. The first draft's `je marche moins vite` was passive and therefore C's voice; eavesdropping on the neighbours' fight has to be a decision. Same reason `se disputent` became `s'engueulent` and `voiture` became `bagnole`: she's not a narrator being polite about her street.
- **No species named in the lyric.** Pictures: she is human; everyone else can be an animal. Don't put a camel in the words. The shisa pouch (`un petit lion rouge`) is the one animal object the lyric already has. Don't add teeth or prey language on top.
- **Verse 1 is a new scene: the 6am machine.** Four lines, no pause, ending on `personne me demande si j'ai dormi` — nobody asks whether I slept. C never had a morning; this version needs one, because the song is about momentum. Three facts that were wrong on the first draft and are now the point of the verse: Aramis is too old to scratch the door, he cries in the kitchen; she drinks tea, not coffee; the boy wants おんぶ (`mon dos`), not 抱っこ (`mes bras`). **Pictures do not put him on her back.** The demand is the line; carrying him through the fights would glue a second body onto every action shot.
- **Verse 2 compresses all four walk sightings into four lines.** C spread them over three separate passes. At 170 that density is authentic to the reference — its verses are fast and wordy on purpose.
- **`la fille veut tout décider` replaced `la fille veut la manette`, which collided with EN.** EN-B verse 4 is the daughter throwing the controller at Kirby; a five-year-old with a game controller in two of the three videos is exactly the repetition the set is built to avoid. FR owns the *Switch* as a possession — `la Switch est à moi, techniquement` in C — and EN owns the controller and the rage. Wanting to run everything is hers in any video and characterises her better anyway.
- **Not `veut commander` — that word has two meanings and the wrong one is commoner.** `Commander` is both *to be in charge* and *to place an order*, and with no object attached the pizza reading wins. `Veut tout décider` can only mean one thing, it's the same syllable count, and it matches how C already describes her: `elle décide de tout`.
- **`veut mon dos` is correct and will survive a French friend but not a machine.** French has no single word for おんぶ, but `vouloir les bras` is genuinely idiomatic for a small child demanding to be picked up — so `veut mon dos` reads instantly as *wants to be carried on my back* to a native ear. A translator renders it as "wants my back" and it looks broken. **Check this one with a person, not an app.**
- **Shipped.** The stills follow D's scenes. C's gelato / Switch / mahjong / 3am milk stay out of them.
- **The bridge is the only place it breathes**, and the reference puts a keys-led breakdown in exactly that slot before the last chorus. `electronic keys`, not acoustic piano — truer to the record, and it leaves piano to EN.
- **`sa niche vide`, not `sa vieille maison`.** C had seven verses to establish the pouch; D's bridge has to introduce it cold, and *his old house* can be heard as an actual house. `Niche` can only be a doghouse, and `vide` carries the fact that it's otherwise empty. `Près de moi` for the same reason — the draft's bare `à côté` never said beside what.
- **The bridge opens on `il` and the only dog named so far is the living one.** That's the intended turn: `il reste dans sa niche vide` sounds like Aramis for exactly one line, then `un petit lion rouge` reveals it isn't. It resolves inside the bridge, so it reads as a reveal rather than a mistake — but it's the one moment in D that depends on the listener waiting a line, so check it lands on take one.
- **Final chorus runs 5 / 9 / 7 / 8 against the main chorus's 5 / 6 / 6 / 7.** Deliberate: it's the lifted last pass and the reference expands there too. If Suno crams `sauf ce soir — le 4 octobre`, that's the line to trim, not the date.
- **`key change` is out of Exclude on purpose.** The reference lifts the final chorus a whole tone and that's part of the effect. Not requested in Style either; `sentimental` and `orchestral strings` still block power-ballad swell.
- **Listen for Japanese phrasing.** A J-rock tag can pull Japanese vowels onto a French vocal. `Japanese lyrics` is excluded and the block is entirely French, but check take one.
- **Check `foutre` on take one.** Suno will sing it, but French vulgarity is where the pronunciation tends to smear, and a mangled swear is worse than no swear. If it's unclear, drop that line rather than re-rolling the whole take for it.
